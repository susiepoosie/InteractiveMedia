Find 3 examples of physical computing — wearables, installations, robots.

Audience by Random International, 2002

A series of mirrors, each individually programmed to display unique ‘behaviours’, silently interacting amongst themselves until a person approaches. Each mirror equipped with a small camera, linked to a computer with motion-tracking software, every mirror turns to face the person in one swift, synchronised motion. In an instant, the onlooker becomes the singular subject of their own gaze from every angle. A beautifully simple example of physical computing that truly fascinated me, the mirrors serving some anthropomorphic function yet so obviously un-human. Similarly, my project will explore the affective relationships between natural and digital worlds.

Smell of Data by Leanne Wijnsma & Froukje Tan, 2026

Drawing inspiration from the 1937 gas leak accident at the New London School in Texas which resulted in hundreds of mortalities, the 'Smell of Data' project aims to warn users of dangers in the digital world, using smell, a sense that previously helped humans to survive in the wild. Wijnsma and Froukje produced a device that releases a noticeable scent upon detecting data leaks. I found it to be such an intriguing and whimsical concept combining physical computing with the senses to create a functional, yet somewhat impractical product. Its origin story surrounding the history of gas leakage accidents was also very interesting and encouraged me to consider how small projects like 'Smell of Data' could reflect broader worldly concerns, when brainstorming for my final project.
